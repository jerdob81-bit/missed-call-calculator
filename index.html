If you buy Google Local Services Ads, Google can charge you for a missed call during your business hours when the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds.

Google's conditions and exceptions

Business hours are the hours in your Google Business Profile, and the call must also fall within your ad schedule if you set one. If your phone menu asks callers to press a key, the 20 seconds start after the key press, and callers who hang up without pressing a key are not charged. Calls that reach voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours are treated as connected and are charged after 20 seconds, whether or not the caller leaves a message. Follow-up calls with the same customer within 15 days are not charged again. Source: Google Local Services Help, "How leads work".

What-if: revenue from leads you did not reach

This is a hypothetical revenue opportunity based on the numbers you enter. It is not measured lost sales, not profit (it does not subtract materials, labor, or software costs), and not an amount a text-back is guaranteed to recover. Formula: leads × percent not reached × assumed close rate × average job.

How missed-call text back works

With HighLevel's Missed Call Text Back turned on, a call to your HighLevel number that is not answered within the ring timeout you set sends the caller an automatic text with a message you write, such as a booking link. HighLevel's help center suggests a ring timeout of about 10 to 20 seconds. The caller gets a written reply they can answer instead of waiting for a callback. Whether that turns into a booked job depends on your message, your follow-up, and the customer.
